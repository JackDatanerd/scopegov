PLAN REQUIREMENT — the two Auth hooks need Supabase Team or Enterprise (not Pro)
  "MFA verification attempt" and "Password verification attempt" are greyed out in Auth -> Hooks on lower
  plans ("Team or Enterprise Plan required"). An earlier draft of this note said the password hook only
  needed Pro; that was wrong. Effects until you upgrade:
    - Nothing breaks. Migration 068 creates hook_mfa_verification_attempt and
      hook_password_verification_attempt anyway; they sit unused. Enabling them later needs NO new
      migration: Auth -> Hooks -> Add hook -> Postgres function -> pick the function.
    - AUTH+MFA #2 below is only HALF closed on a lower plan. Closed on every plan: sign-ins are audited
      server-side by the auth.sessions trigger. OPEN until the hooks are on: someone who already holds a
      victim's password can guess authenticator codes by calling Supabase Auth directly (the in-app
      lockout only sees calls made through /api/auth/mfa/*), limited only by Supabase's own rate limits.
    - Failed sign-ins are not audited and password sign-in has no per-account lockout (both come from the
      password hook). Supabase's per-IP limits still apply.
    - Meanwhile: Auth -> Rate Limits, keep the verification limits tight. Everything else in this round
      (atomic in-app limiter, step-up, backup-code hardening, owner protection...) works on every plan.

AUTH + MFA — bugs fixed
 1 HIGH  Onboarding deadlock. Once /api/workspace/create made the user an Owner (MFA-mandatory), the
         enrolment gate 401'd every call the onboarding wizard makes (branding, defaults, invite,
         complete-onboarding...). Every new owner was stuck on step 1 with no link to /mfa-setup.
         middleware.ts now keeps the onboarding APIs reachable until onboarding completes; the person
         is then sent to /mfa-setup.
 2 HIGH  MFA lockout was only enforced in the app's own route. A password-only session can call GoTrue's
         MFA endpoints directly with the public anon key. hook_mfa_verification_attempt (and the password
         hook) enforce it inside Auth — BUT ONLY ONCE ENABLED, WHICH NEEDS THE TEAM PLAN (see PLAN
         REQUIREMENT), so on a lower plan the direct-call gap stays open. What IS closed on every plan:
         sign-ins were audited only when the BROWSER reported them; a trigger on auth.sessions now records
         every sign-in (app-side reporting stays as a fallback and stands down when the trigger already
         wrote the row).
 3 MED   /api/auth/reset-password accepted ANY session that had authenticated in the last 15 minutes
         (an ordinary password login, a hijacked cookie) — bypassing change-password's current-password
         check, throttle and audit. It now requires a recovery / OTP / magic-link session.
 4 MED   The attempt limiter was check-then-act: N parallel guesses all passed the check. Now a single
         SQL function (advisory lock + reserve + count) — exactly `max` attempts reach the credential
         check however many arrive at once (tested: 5 of 40 parallel).
 5 MED   middleware.ts built its response before the session refresh, so the route handler after it
         received the OLD cookies and re-refreshed with a token the middleware had just consumed.
         The response is now rebuilt when cookies rotate.
 6 LOW   Backup codes: retiring the old set could fail silently while the UI said it worked, and two
         concurrent regenerations left both sets valid -> one transaction (issue_backup_codes).
         Codes are 12 chars (was 10) and stored as HMAC-SHA256(MFA_BACKUP_CODE_PEPPER) instead of an
         unsalted SHA-256 (a database leak made all codes brute-forceable in hours). Old codes still work.
 7 LOW   OAuth/PKCE callback logged every `code` exchange as method 'google'; now derived from the session.
 8 LOW   change-password decided "has a password" from the identities list (unreliable for OAuth-first
         users); now reads the real credential (user_has_password).
 9 LOW   MFA enrolment could be completed from a password-only session while another factor was verified.
10 LOW   Invite acceptance sits under a public middleware prefix and skipped the second-factor gate.
11 LOW   Passwords: rejects single-character, very common and email-as-password.

RLS + PERMISSIONS — bugs fixed
 1 MED   Invites outlived their sender: deactivating, removing or demoting the inviter left their pending
         invites valid (and Resend refreshed them). They are revoked on deactivation/leave, and re-checked
         at acceptance against the sender's CURRENT authority. Resend now applies the role ceiling.
 2 MED   Admin "Reset MFA" removed a credential that guards the person's access to EVERY workspace, from
         one workspace, without a step-up, leaving their live sessions alive. Now: the actor confirms
         first; the target's sessions are revoked; every workspace the person belongs to gets an audit
         row (without naming the acting admin to the others).
 3 MED   DELETE /api/workspace/delete needed only MANAGE_WORKSPACE_SETTINGS (a routinely delegated
         permission) yet cancels billing and deactivates everyone. Owner-only (unless the owner is no
         longer a member), the typed name is verified server-side, and it needs a step-up.
 4 MED   public.users.name could be rewritten straight through PostgREST, skipping every sanitiser
         (5,000 chars, newlines, markup). CHECK constraint added; the direct write grant removed.
 5 LOW   The workspace Owner was protected only by an accident: the Owner role held a stale EXPORT_DATA
         key no other role could hold. The key is removed and the protection is explicit
         (lib/utils/owner-protection.ts): nobody else can deactivate the owner or change their role/overrides.
 6 LOW   Grant hygiene: cron_heartbeats still granted ALL to anon/authenticated; a sweep now closes any
         drift and default privileges stop new tables inheriting API-role grants. logos / flag-evidence
         buckets get size and mime limits (and are created if missing).
 7 LOW   Security events about a person (MFA on/off, backup codes, password, email change, admin reset)
         are audited in every workspace they belong to, not just the active one.
 8 LOW   getMembersWithPermission / role-holder lookups were capped at 500; now paged.
 9 GAP   Onboarding invited people with no role, so each got the workspace default (as seeded: all
         projects, financials, client data, and mandatory MFA). The wizard now shows and sends a role.
Already closed by the Settings+Team pass (79d17d1), covered by regression tests here: invite role pinned
+ ceiling-checked at invite time; role PATCH floor applies to isDefault; unique role names; role/permission
change audit diffs; roster MFA badge.

FEATURES BUILT
 - Step-up ("confirm it's you"): password or authenticator code, valid 10 minutes, bound to the session.
   Guards: disable MFA, regenerate backup codes, delete account (typing your own email proved nothing),
   delete workspace, transfer ownership, admin MFA reset, cancel billing, change sign-in email.
   Client: fetchWithStepUp() + <StepUpHost/> in the app layout.
 - Sessions list (device, IP, last active) with per-session sign-out (Settings -> Account).
 - New-device sign-in alerts (email + in-app + audit) when a browser/OS not seen in 90 days signs in.
 - Guarded sign-in email change (Settings -> Account), notice to the old address, audit rows.
 - Server-side sign-in audit (all plans). Failed-sign-in audit + lockout: built, but it lives in the
   password hook, which needs Team/Enterprise (see PLAN REQUIREMENT).

CAVEATS
 - Not run against a live GoTrue/PostgREST. The recovery-session rule denies known non-mailbox methods
   (password, oauth, sso) rather than requiring one specific `amr` name, so it can't lock legitimate
   resets out if GoTrue names the method differently.
 - The auth.sessions triggers need a role that owns that table; if creation is refused the migration
   warns and the app-side login-event fallback stays active.
 - (Once the hooks are enabled on Team) the MFA hook lockout can be triggered by anyone who holds the password (they can keep the victim out of
   the second-factor step for a few minutes; they still can't get in).
